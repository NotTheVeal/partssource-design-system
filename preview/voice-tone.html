Voice & Tone

PartsSource is a clinical-grade procurement partner. Copy carries a single unifying promise — Ensuring healthcare is always on® — and every button, error, headline, and email subject line should reflect it. Sourced from the PartsSource Copywriting Best Practices for Healthcare Communication and the CTA Audit Report — PartsSource Platform.

The promise

Every sentence should answer one question.
Tagline

Ensuring healthcare is always on®

Does this help keep healthcare running — reliably, safely, and without disruption? If not, cut it.

What copy must do

Three non-negotiables from the Copywriting Best Practices guide.
1

Reduce cognitive load

Busy healthcare professionals scan; they don't read. One idea per sentence. One action per button. Progressive disclosure over up-front explanation.

2

Build trust in high-stakes environments

Patient safety, compliance, and uptime are at stake. Write with authority, never hype. Let outcomes and proof points do the work.

3

Drive confident action

Keep equipment, systems, and care delivery operational. If a sentence doesn't help the user decide, act, or reduce risk — cut it.

We are / we are not

Four traits and their opposites. Lean toward the "we are" column when in doubt.
We are

Clear over clever

Plain language. Say what the thing is before what it does.

We are not

"A seamless, end-to-end procurement experience." Marketing fluff. Vague euphemism.

We are

Confident, not arrogant

"Trusted by thousands of hospitals to manage medical equipment parts."

We are not

"The best platform in healthcare." Hype. Indefensible claim.

We are

Human, not casual

Smart colleague explaining something. Natural sentence structure.

We are not

Slang, emojis, internet shorthand, or playful tone in critical moments.

We are

Specific, never silent

"Reduce part sourcing time by consolidating vendors in one platform."

We are not

"Save time and money." Generic adjectives without data, outcome, or constraint.

Button labels · Verb–Subject is non-negotiable

The naming pattern from the CTA Audit. Verb first, then the object it affects. Mirrors natural language, reduces cognitive load (Hick's Law), accelerates recognition (Jakob's Law).
PatternWhen to useExample
View DetailsThe action opens a single item or record — card → detail."View Details" on an asset card · part card · event card
View All + [Object]The action navigates to a full list or collection — section → list."View All Events" · "View All Quotes" · "View All Orders"
Verb + ObjectWorkflow or task-based action."Submit Work Order" · "Approve Event" · "Request Quote"
Show All / Expand · CollapseSection disclosure. Make the expand/collapse state explicit."Show All" → "Expand View" / "Collapse View"
CancelAlways "Cancel". Never "Nevermind", "Back", or "Discard" for a cancel."Cancel" (secondary button in confirm dialogs)
Destructive verbName the destruction. Never "OK" or "Confirm" for a destructive action."Delete List" · "Remove Item" · "Discard Draft"

Primary vs. Secondary CTAs

One Primary per screen or section. From the Copywriting guide §10.4.
TierWhen to useExamples
Primary One per screen / section. Reserved for the highest-value action. Must directly support uptime, readiness, or operational clarity. "Resolve Open Repairs" · "Submit Order" · "Review Critical Alerts"
Secondary Supports learning or exploration. Follows the same Verb–Subject rules. Must never compete visually with the primary. "View Details" · "Learn More" · "Download Overview"

CTA anti-patterns · do not use

Rule of thumb: users must know what will happen before they commit. From §10.4.
Don't · noun only
"Report" · "Form" · "Dashboard"No verb. User has no idea what clicking does.
Don't · vague action
"Click here" · "Continue" · "Get started"Doesn't name the outcome. Defaults to fluff.
Don't · marketing-first
"Discover" · "Explore" · "Unlock"Without a concrete object, sounds like a brochure, not a tool.
Don't · overloaded
"Save & Continue to Review and Submit"Multiple actions in one button. Pick one.
Do · urgency, calm
"Address Overdue Repairs" · "Approve Pending Orders"Verb-first. Operational. Names the outcome.
Don't · alarmist
"Fix this now!" · "Don't miss out!"Emotional pressure erodes trust in clinical contexts.

Audience-first writing

From §3. Start with the user's job, respect their expertise.
1

Start with the job

Lead with the pain point or job-to-be-done. Introduce the product only after context is set. Problem → why it matters → how PartsSource helps.

2

Respect domain expertise

Most users are SMEs. Don't over-explain industry fundamentals. Do explain what's different or what's changed. Avoid condescension and teaching tone.

3

Lead with insight, not promotion

From §6. Credibility comes from perspective. Reference PartsSource only where it naturally supports the point.

One-pager structure

From §10. The five-section shape every brochureware piece must follow.
1

Value headline

What it is + why it matters. Stands alone without body copy.

2

Operational context

The problem being solved. Use the language of the buyer's daily work.

3

Proof or scale

Concrete metrics, adoption, outcomes. Label projections clearly. No "significant savings" without context.

4

Functional breakdown

What the solution actually does. Plain, operational language.

5

Clear next step

One Verb–Subject CTA. The one action you want the reader to take.

The final principle

From §12 of the Copywriting guide.
Why we write

When systems fail, healthcare stops. Our job is to make sure it doesn't.

If the copy does not support that outcome, it does not belong.

Common patterns — Do & Don't

Pick once, use everywhere.
Do
"We couldn't submit your order because the part number is missing. Add a part number and try again."Names what happened · why · what to do next.
Don't
"Something went wrong."No specifics. No recovery. Erodes trust under stress.
Do
"Delete this list?" (confirm dialog title) → button "Delete List"Subject matches between title and primary CTA.
Don't
"Are you sure you want to permanently remove this item?" → "OK"Hedging title. Generic button.
Do
"Saved." (toast)One word. The user already knows what they saved.
Don't
"Your changes have been successfully saved!"Length + exclamation = treat user like a child.
Do
"Trusted by thousands of hospitals to manage medical equipment parts."Authority through proof.
Don't
"The best platform in healthcare."Hype. Indefensible. Erodes confidence.
